# Rolling sounds

Add a **Rolling Sound** component to the table root. Assign a default ball sound material for older or untagged ball prefabs, then choose the table, playfield and ramp gains. Each ball owns its loop voices; rolling speed updates their gain, pitch and position without restarting the clip. Rolling uses actual supporting physics contacts, so airborne balls, side walls, covers and captured balls do not produce a rolling loop.

Assign a **Ball Sound Material** to each ball prefab's `BallComponent`. Steel and Ceramic are separate acoustic identities, independent of mass, appearance and magnet behavior. The tag follows the prefab through trough capture, drain and recreation. An explicitly tagged ball does not inherit the table's default tag. A missing ball/surface response is silent and reports a warning once per pair; an optional fallback must be assigned explicitly on the surface material.

Assign a **Rolling Sound Material** to a physics material to share its acoustic identity. Local numerical physics overrides still inherit that sound assignment. To override one item, add **Rolling Sound Surface** to the same GameObject as the collidable item and select Inherit, Override or Silent. Overrides do not search ancestor names or change friction, elasticity or scatter. Local support direction is optional and intended for mechanisms that support balls against a wall or inverted surface; ordinary ramps and rails should use table gravity.

Each surface material contains a response row for each ball material. A row supplies a looping `SoundEffectAsset`, optional non-looping Enter and Exit banks, speed-dependent gain/pitch, fade times and boundary cooldowns. The default speed is the ball center's tangential speed relative to its supporting surface, including moving mechanisms; contact slip speed is intentionally not used. Integer Horizontal speed and frequency-offset pitch modes support VPX-style tables. The T2 presets preserve its cubic gain and quadratic/linear pitch formulas. Boundary sounds play across a material change: Exit from the old surface, then Enter on the new one, at the recorded crossing point. Stopping and restarting on the same surface does not retrigger them. Air arrival/departure sounds are disabled by default because collision sounds already own ball landings. Turn off **Material Transition Sounds** for a strict comparison with T2's VPX script, which does not use dedicated boundary one-shots. Capture, drain, spawn and teardown remain silent cleanup events.

The library's `Assets/Sounds/Rolling` folder contains Wood, Plastic Ramp and Wire Rails surfaces, tagged physics materials, functional support templates and a Ceramic ball prefab. Steel responses use the extracted T2 samples. The Ceramic responses and short wood/plastic seam sounds are explicitly labeled synthesized starting points, not recordings of a Powerball; substitute measured recordings as they become available. The wire entry/exit banks use T2's additional wire samples. Original stereo recordings, cooking hashes and synthesis provenance are retained in `Sources~`. Playback assets are already mono PCM WAVs, so runtime packages do not depend on Unity import-time channel conversion.

Export includes every reachable response row, ball tag, bank and clip, including Ceramic rows when the current table spawns only Steel. The optional `table/rolling/graph.json` payload preserves shared identities and references to normal packaged sound and physics assets. Runtime import reconstructs the graph before restoring component references and owns the transient assets until table unload. Older readers can skip the rolling payload and rolling components. Rolling audio does not require the authoring asset library to be installed in the player.

For a custom table, begin with the library support templates or tagged physics materials, retain the table's existing physical coefficients, and tune the sound response separately. Supporting rails that share one rolling material produce one loop per ball. Lateral guides should remain unassigned unless contact tracing shows that they actually support the ball.
